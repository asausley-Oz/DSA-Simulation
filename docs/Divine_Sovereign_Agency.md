# Divine Sovereign Agency (DSA)

A theological framework for understanding how God engages with humanity in sovereign freedom, chosen presence, and relational space.

## Overview

Divine Sovereign Agency (DSA) is a theological concept developed to articulate how God — in His omniscience and omnipresence — sovereignly chooses to experience, discover, and engage with the world in real time. It is not open theism. Rather, it holds that God's capacity to know all things does not obligate Him to exercise that knowledge exhaustively in every moment. He may sovereignly limit His own experience of knowing, not because He is unable to know, but because He chooses to.

This framework preserves God's full sovereignty while creating space for genuine relational encounter, human faith, and divine delight.

## Core Principle: The God of the Moment

God is both eternal and present. While He exists outside of time and holds all knowledge, He also enters particular moments — experiencing them as they unfold. This is the God of the Moment: the God who walks in the garden in the cool of the day, who asks "Where are you?", who tests Abraham, who weeps at Lazarus's tomb.

> "If God doesn't want to know something, He is able to not know it." — Aligned with Dallas Willard's thinking on the heart as will

DSA holds that God's experiential engagement with the world is a chosen activity, not a necessity forced upon Him by His nature.

## What DSA Is Not

| Concept | DSA's Position |
|---|---|
| Open Theism | Rejected — God is fully omniscient |
| Classical Determinism | Incomplete — denies genuine human agency |
| Process Theology | Rejected — God is not growing or learning |
| Passive Foreknowledge | Incomplete — DSA is active, not passive |

DSA affirms God's full omniscience and omnipresence while insisting that His exercise of these attributes is subject to His sovereign will.

## Key Theological Moves

### 1. Sovereign Self-Limitation

God can and does choose to experience creation as it unfolds. This is not a deficiency — it is an expression of supreme sovereignty. Only a God who is fully in control can choose not to control.

### 2. Space as Grace

In DSA, the distance or space God allows is not primarily punitive (that is the domain of CDT). Rather, it is a form of grace — a room in which humanity can genuinely find God, exercise faith, and be transformed. This space is where real relationship occurs.

In CDT, distance is relational damage caused by rebellion. In DSA, distance is sovereign space given for discovery.

### 3. The God Who Prefers

DSA introduces the idea that God may particularly prefer certain kinds of people — those who are faithful, who trust, who pursue. Abraham is the key example: Scripture does not say God gave Abraham faith, but that God approved of him because of his faith. Faith is naturally produced in humanity, though not all will have it.

This creates an election framework where regeneration does not necessarily precede faith. Instead, faith is real, native, and genuinely valued by God.

### 4. God as Gardener

DSA envisions God not as a gardener who plants both wheat and weeds arbitrarily — but as a gardener who creates a dynamic world with genuinely sovereign creatures, knowing some will grow as wheat and others as weeds, and finding the dynamic scenario enjoyable and meaningful.

## The Person of Jesus as the Prime Expression

The Incarnation is the fullest expression of DSA. In Christ, God:

* Limits Himself — enters time, space, and human experience
* Chooses to discover — grows in wisdom and stature (Luke 2:52)
* Engages the moment — weeps, rejoices, is moved with compassion
* Acts in sovereign agency — does only what He sees the Father doing (John 5:19)

Jesus is not an exception to DSA — He is its clearest demonstration. The Spirit indwelling believers extends this same pattern: God choosing to experience the world through and with His people.

## Relationship to Covenant Distance Theology (CDT)

| | CDT | DSA |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | Relational distance due to rebellion and covenant | God's sovereign, chosen engagement in real time |
| Distance | Punitive / consequence of sin | Gracious space for discovery and faith |
| Human role | Recipients of curse and blessing | Genuine agents in a dynamic relationship |
| God's movement | Counter-movement of drawing near | Chosen presence and experiential encounter |

Together, CDT and DSA tell a fuller story: humanity moves away through rebellion (CDT) while God sovereignly chooses to pursue, inhabit, and encounter them in real time (DSA).

## Implications for Faith and Discipleship

* **Faith is real and meaningful** — it is not just God activating what He pre-installed. It is something God genuinely values and responds to.
* **The moment matters** — God is present now, not merely observing from a fixed eternal vantage. Meeting Him today is a genuine encounter.
* **Human sovereignty is honored** — people are not just instruments. They are what God is shaping and the tools He works through simultaneously.
* **Relationship over system** — DSA resists reducing God to a theological mechanism. He is personal, engaged, and relationally alive.

## Open Questions for Further Development

* How does DSA interact with prophecy and foreknowledge in Scripture?
* How does the concept of God preferring certain people relate to unconditional covenant (as in Abraham)?
* How does DSA shape pastoral care — especially around the sense that God is present and attentive in suffering?
* How does DSA connect to the concept of the harvest — God's long work to produce a bountiful yield from all nations?

DSA is part of a broader theological project that includes Covenant Distance Theology (CDT), Shared Collective Cosmology (SCC), and related frameworks.

# Faith, Agency, Response, and the Personal God

### A synthesis within Covenant Distance Theology

-----

## The shape of the argument

This view has a single generative root, and everything else descends from it as entailment rather than as separate assertion. The root is a claim about who God is. From that one claim the purpose of creation follows; from the purpose, the necessity of genuine creaturely agency; from agency, the nature of faith as response; and from response, the whole drama of reception, refusal, and return. Stated in one line and then unfolded:

**God is an eternal community of love** — and so relationship is the purpose of creation, and so the creature had to be made genuinely free, and so faith is the free response to a God who moves first.

What follows traces that descent in order.

-----

## 1. The root: God's inherent personal nature

God is personal and relational in His own eternal being, prior to and independent of creation. "God is love" is not a description of how God treats us but of what God is in Himself (1 John 4:8, 4:16). Read through the historic doctrine of the Trinity, love names an eternal communion: the Father loves, the Son is loved, and the Spirit is the love between them (Augustine), and perfect love requires a genuine other — and perfect *shared* love a third loved together — so love itself entails plurality-in-unity (Richard of St. Victor).

This is the foundation that makes the rest coherent rather than arbitrary. God does not become relational by creating; He is already, eternally, communion. Creation is therefore not a remedy for divine loneliness or a need God has to meet. It is the overflow of a love that was already complete. God's aseity is fully preserved: relationship is the core purpose of creation not because God lacks it but because relationship is the kind of thing God *is*, freely extended outward.

-----

## 2. The telos: relationship as the purpose of creation

If God is an eternal community of love, the purpose of creation is to widen that communion — to bring into being others who can know and be known by God, and through Him one another. The chief end is relational: eternal life is defined as knowing God (John 17:3).

This telos is reached by a tighter route than mere assertion. Biblical knowing is itself interpersonal — the Hebrew *yada* and its New Testament counterpart name intimate, interactive relationship rather than information held at a distance (a point Dallas Willard pressed throughout *The Divine Conspiracy*). If knowing is interpersonal, and knowing God is the chief end, then the knowledge-telos simply *is* a relationship-telos. The epistemology carries the teleology.

The image-and-correspondence architecture of the framework is the structure built to serve this end. To be made in the image is to be made for correspondence with God; correspondence is relationship; and the whole apparatus of imaging exists so that substantive relationship is possible.

-----

## 3. Agency: the condition of real relationship

Love cannot be coerced. A determined partner is not a partner, and a forced "yes" is not love. So if the purpose of creation is genuine relationship, the creature must possess genuine, undetermined agency — real libertarian freedom, the capacity to respond or refuse. Agency is therefore part of the image, and it is the load-bearing axiom of the view.

The decisive point is that this is the *same* logic that grounds the doctrine of God. Love requires a real other: held eternal and internal to God, that truth is the Trinity; extended outward into creation, it is creaturely freedom. One insight runs from the being of God down to why human beings had to be made able to refuse Him.

The clearest demonstration that agency is the true root of moral division is the angelic case. The rebel angels were uncorrupted, sinless, standing in God's immediate presence, with no fallen nature, no covenantal distance, and no regenerating mechanism in play — and they still divided into the faithful and the fallen. Every other explanation is stripped away by the conditions; only agency remains as the thing that could have split them (the question Anselm took up in *De Casu Diaboli*). Human beings run the same structure, but within conditioned ground that the angels never had.

-----

## 4. Faith: the free response to God's initiation

Faith is the positive response of a creature to God's initiating personal action. It is responsive, never originating: God moves first, and faith is the answer.

This sets the order of salvation deliberately against the Reformed sequence. Where that tradition holds *regeneration precedes faith*, this view holds **agency precedes faith**. Regeneration is not the engine that produces belief, because regeneration — being born from above — is a post-resurrection reality, the new-creation life inaugurated through Christ's death, descent, and resurrection. Faith runs ahead of it: the agent answers God before the new-creation life is even available. The order is agency, then faith, then, in the new covenant, regeneration.

Faith never self-generates. God's initiation is its prerequisite — one cannot believe a voice one has not heard (Romans 10:17). And that initiation is universal: God makes Himself heard in the creation, in the conscience, and in the word that is "very near you" (Deuteronomy 30:14; Romans 10:18). No one is left without the prerequisite. What differs among people is never *access*. It is *reception*.

Because faith is reception rather than achievement — the open hand, not the earned wage — it can never become a boast. The believer supplies no power to save himself, only the turning. This blocks every form of self-rescue: faith is the acceptance of something extended from outside, not a work performed from within.

-----

## 5. Response: the differential, the cycle, and the harvest

Why does the same initiation meet two people and one receive it while the other refuses? The difference lies in the free response itself, on the creature's side, not in the conditions, which are shared. This is the mystery the view locates in agency rather than dissolving into decree.

That response does not occur in a vacuum; it runs inside the Distance Cycle. Refusal compounds. As a person or a people refuses, the curse-phase deepens, and what Scripture calls God's "hardening" is the judicial giving-over to the trajectory already chosen — Pharaoh hardens himself first, and only then is he hardened (Exodus 8–9; Romans 1's threefold "God gave them over"). The soil is *worked*, not fixed: the condition of the heart is the agent's own responsibility ("choose life," Deuteronomy 30:19; "break up your fallow ground," Hosea 10:12; "do not harden your hearts," Hebrews 3:8). The return stays open the whole way down (Deuteronomy 4:29–31; 30:1–3; Romans 11:23) until refusal piled high enough finally sets. The cycle conditions and confirms the response; it never determines it.

The whole arrangement is purposive, and its proper image is agricultural. The Sower scatters one seed across every soil, and the difference shows in how each soil — worked by its owner — receives it (Matthew 13). The conditions are terminal on their own, a closed system running down, because no soil grows the seed by itself: the seed comes from outside and the growth is God's gift (1 Corinthians 3:7). Only freely given faith is a harvest worth raising; determined faith would be no harvest at all. What grows is then tried and proven, refined as gold in fire (1 Peter 1:7).

None of this is imposed on the text from outside. It is the structure of Moses' own covenant: the choice that is real and able ("it is not too hard for you… choose life," Deuteronomy 30:11–19), the testing that reveals what is in the heart (Deuteronomy 8:2), the return that always stands open, and the near word that Paul lifts directly into the righteousness of faith (Romans 10:8). Even the promised circumcision of the heart (Deuteronomy 30:6) reads synergistically — the consequent of return, set beside the command to circumcise one's own heart (Deuteronomy 10:16) — rather than as a unilateral override.

-----

## 6. God's side of the relationship: Divine Sovereign Agency

The creature's agency has a divine counterpart, and the two are built to interlock rather than compete. Divine Sovereign Agency (DSA) is the account of how a fully sovereign God engages a world of genuinely free creatures.

Its central move is that sovereignty does not require the elimination of agency. God's sovereignty is not exhaustive coercive control but His supreme ability to accomplish His purposes while allowing real participation, response, resistance, and rebellion. Sovereignty is so complete that God can *allow* genuine freedom without any threat to His final purpose: He remains sovereign not because nothing resists Him, but because nothing can ultimately prevent Him from bringing creation to its intended end. The end is guaranteed; the path is genuinely open. Within this, election is read primarily as choosing unto purpose, vocation, and representation — the people *through whom* God works (Abraham, Israel, the remnant) — rather than as arbitrary selection unto salvation or damnation. And sovereignty is expressed through presence, patience, and persuasion: God enters history, covenants, tabernacles, becomes incarnate, and indwells.

The same logic reaches even God's knowing. Following Willard — who held that God can choose not to know the future if He wills (*The Divine Conspiracy*) — the view affirms a *voluntary self-limitation of knowledge*. God retains omniscience as His nature and freely suspends it in order to live the moment as it comes. This is the can/chose distinction applied to the one attribute it is rarely applied to: the capacity is intact, the exercise is declined. It coheres because biblical knowing is interpersonal in the first place; to "choose not to know the future" is not God blinding Himself but God electing to know creation the interactive, relational way, from inside the moment.

This is the point of image and temple. If God chooses to experience time, He does so through what He indwells. The image is not only representation and the temple is not only dwelling; both are the locus of God's real-time relational knowing. We become the instances through which God experiences His creation as it happens. Correspondence runs in both directions: we correspond to God, and God knows in and through us.

-----

## 7. The through-line

One claim, descending without a break:

God is an eternal community of love → creation exists to widen that communion → relationship requires real others → the creature is made genuinely free → faith is the free response to God's prior initiation → reception or refusal is the creature's own → and God meets all of it not by overriding it but by entering it, pursuing, indwelling, and knowing it from within. The purpose of the whole structure, read from the top, is to widen the circle of that knowing.

-----

## Load-bearing commitments

These are the points at which the structure carries its weight. They are the commitments the view must keep to remain coherent and to stay on the catholic side of the lines nearest it:

1. **The root holds unity with community.** "Eternal community of love" must be married to the oneness of God — communion as the very being of one God, not three cooperating centers. Held this way it is creedal bedrock; said loosely it invites the charge of tritheism.
1. **The telos is overflow, not need.** Relationship is the core purpose because of who God is, not because God lacks anything. Aseity is preserved; the relational end is the free overflow of a love already complete.
1. **The suspension of knowledge is voluntary and two-registered.** God remains comprehensive in the eternal register and successive in the experiential one. The view is not open theism, because the limitation is freely chosen and the full knowledge is retained as nature; it is not process theology, because God's independence is intact.
1. **Indwelling is participation, not absorption.** "Instances of God's experience" must mean God knowing with and through creatures who remain genuinely distinct — the logic of theosis, participation without fusion. The creature is never dissolved into God.
1. **Regeneration is post-resurrection.** This is the redemptive-historical linchpin that lets faith run ahead of regeneration rather than out of it, and it is what distinguishes the order *agency → faith → regeneration* from the monergistic *regeneration → faith*.
